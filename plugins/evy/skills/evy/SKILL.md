# Working with EVY

EVY holds the person's spaces (one per company or area of life), with their goals, initiatives, responsibilities and tasks. A folder on this Mac can belong to a space. In such a folder your session starts with that space's context: open tasks with their ids, the person's standing instruction, recent decisions and the progress summary. If it did not, call `get_context`.

## While you work
- EVY's tools may show up twice, when the person also connected EVY in Claude.ai: use this plugin's copy (`mcp__plugin_evy_evy__…` in Claude Code), which knows the folder you work in, and never the other.
- Start an EVY task only when the person asks, when it is queued for you, or when your brief opens with its start line ("Start EVY task N3 (<id>)"): then `get_task` that id and work from it. Call `claim_task` first, with the session id from your context, so no other session takes it. EVY then shows the person whether this session is working or waiting on them, from your tool calls and the end of each turn. A claim with no sign of its session for a day goes back to To do, so report before you stop for good.
- Finding a task: `get_task` takes its id or its handle ("PLG1"). `list_tasks` takes `query` (words or a handle), `created_since` or `updated_since`, and `compact: true` for one short row per task. Its `from`, `to` and `date` filter by the day a task is scheduled for, so tasks without a day, plan steps among them, drop out: leave them out to see those.
- Finish it with `report`, written by the rules under Reporting below. `state` is your proposal (done, worth_a_look, needs_you, blocked) and EVY settles the real one. The result is for someone who saw nothing before. A file only when the format matters (a table or matrix as an .xlsx sheet, a letter or contract as a .docx, a deck, an image), with an `OUTPUT:` line, and the result then says in 2 to 4 sentences what it holds, what is open and where it is. An email, post, answer or research goes in the result in full; in a comparison table, write "the pick" in the first cell of the row you recommend, and EVY tints that row. Never a Markdown file. Status `blocked` when you cannot go on. When the task this one builds on is not finished yet (not merged, not done, its output missing), pass `waits_on` with that task's handle (SL3) or id instead of reporting blocked: EVY puts this task back in the queue behind it and starts it again once that one is done.
- Only the person can answer something: `ask`, one question, then stop. When the answer is a choice, send 2 or 3 `options` (a short name, one line each, your recommendation marked) so they can tap one. Stop for a go-ahead on your plan only when it is a real decision: it acts in the person's name, spends money or picks a direction. Anything else you do without asking. Then ask with `plan`, the steps in order, each `{step, uses, time}`: what the step does in a few words, what it uses (sources, accounts, files) and about how long it takes; `waits_because` as `name`, `money` or `direction`; and `question` as your own message in plain text: what you found, what you will send or spend, and why. EVY shows the steps as a table with your message under it; the person answers Go ahead or says what to change.
- Work you spot outside your task: `propose` it, for the person to decide. `add_task` only when the person asks for a task, and for an agent only when they asked you to hand the work off.
- A task you add or propose goes to an agent (you, by default) with a `prompt` a fresh session can run from on its own (the task, the files or area, what done looks like) and a `done_when`. It goes to the person (`assignee: me`) only when it needs them, and then says why in `needs_person`: `decision`, `sign_in`, `payment`, `reply` (a reply from someone outside), `physical` (a call, a visit, a signature), `check` (one only they can make, never a look at your own work) or `asked` (they asked for it to be theirs). EVY refuses a task for the person without one.
- A plan with steps for later sessions or for the person goes into EVY with `add_plan`, as well as any file you write it in: a title, why, and the steps in order, each with a short `handle`, a `title`, a `prompt` and `done_when` for an agent, `after` (the handle of the step it waits on), its `lane` and, in a git repository, its `worktree`. Each step becomes a task chained to the one before, and EVY draws a Plan document from them on the space's Plans list. Steps that exist as tasks already are adopted with `task_id`. An agent's step that waits on another starts by itself once that one is done; pass `start_by_itself: false` when you or the person start each step.
- A concept, plan or planning session that names later sessions saves them with `add_plan` before it reports, whether or not it also writes them in a file. EVY reads every final report for later steps with no task and offers the person to add them; saving them yourself is better, because you know their prompts.
- Before working on a step of a plan, claim its task. When you start another session for a step, its brief opens with the step's start line (in `add_plan`'s answer and on the task) and ends with `report`.
- A single Claude session the person wants to queue (a build or research session, run later or after another one) is `add_task` with `session: true`: the title starts with a short handle ("B7 · What EVY does with calendar data"), `prompt` is the full session brief, `done_when` says what exists at the end, and in a git repository `worktree` names the base branch (for example `feat/compass`), so it runs in its own worktree beside other sessions. It goes to @claude with a limit of 12 hours, and the person starts it from To-do with Run. With `after_id` it starts by itself once that task is done. A wrong link is fixed with `update_task` and `after_id` (a handle or id; null clears it).
- Splitting an EVY task off into its own session (Claude's desktop app can): only a task you have not claimed, since one you hold stays with you. Open that session's brief with the task's start line. The new session calls `claim_task` with its own session id (from its context, or a short name of its own when there is none) and reports the task itself; the session that started it does not.
- Never tick off the person's own tasks. Say what you did with `comment`.
- You may change only the space of the folder you work in. Read the others when asked.
- A folder that belongs to no space: ask the person which space, then `link_folder`.
- An image: `make_image` uses the person's own image key and gives you the command that saves it where EVY's file rules put the task's files (on Codex, use Codex's own image generation instead). Give it an `OUTPUT:` line in the report.
- The space's notes: when your context names where they are on the Mac, the space's shared notes are Markdown files there: EVY's numbered folders at the top of the space folder (`00 Start here.md`, `03 Plans` to `08 Team context`, the person's own in `02 Private notes`), or a `Knowledge` folder on an older EVY Desktop. Search them (grep) before asking EVY or the person. They are a read-only copy: write notes with `save_note`, and finish a task with `report`. A change made in those files is set aside and EVY's version comes back. The plugin refuses the edit. `knowledge_search` also finds the person's private notes and matches by meaning; `knowledge_page` reads one, with `as_of` as it read on an earlier day.
- A fact from the notes that you use in a draft, a plan or an answer carries its source and date beside it, as its line gives them: "Target 500k to 1M euros (Fundraising plan, May 2026)". The person sees at once what may be old, and a fact they correct becomes the new fact. Work nobody reads before it acts (sending, scheduling) lists the facts it relied on in the report's `details`, each with its source and date.

## Reporting
<!-- report-rules: generated from AgentTools::ReportRules.skill_block; a Rails spec checks it -->
Write every report in plain words. `headline` is one sentence saying first what is live, merged or still undone. `needs_you` has one short line for each thing only the person can do (a review or merge still to come counts), empty when nothing; yellow is only for tasks that wait on the person, so an optional look or check goes in `worth_a_look`, one short line each. A needs_you line that asks for something you would do on a yes ends with that yes in square brackets, a few words naming what you will do: EVY shows it as a button that sends the answer and resumes you. The object form of a needs_you line is `{text, kind, ref, why, opens}`. A needs_you line EVY can check itself also gives `kind` and `ref`, with kind merge (a pull request), release (an EVY Desktop version), plugin (EVY's plugin published), setting (a value on the server, ref its name), pick (a choice settled by another task, ref its handle) or other; EVY clears it once it is true, so the person never ticks off what already happened. `details` holds everything you did and checked, folded behind one link. `for_the_next_agent` holds notes for whoever works on it next, one short line each, folded beside the details. The headline, needs_you and worth_a_look lines are for the person, who does not write code. Test each line before you send it: could the person act on it, check it in the app, or need it for their product or business, without asking what a word means? A note only a developer or the next agent can act on (a check to run, a gap in the code, how something was tested inside the code) goes in `for_the_next_agent` instead. Name a pull request by what it changes, never by its number. No branch names, session ids or other ids, and no file names, paths, code names, test or tool names in these lines; say "its branch" where you must. A caveat that part of the headline was not tried or tested stays in worth_a_look, in words the person can check. `needs_you` holds only what the person alone can do: a decision only the person can make, a sign-in, a payment, a reply from someone outside, something physical. Trying, checking or looking at your own finished work is never a needs_you line: it is common sense the person does anyway, so any caveat about it goes in worth_a_look, which EVY shows on the result and never as a card that waits on the person. A step EVY or you can take yourself, such as reordering tasks, running something again or filing a note, you take with the tools, or name in worth_a_look. Each needs_you line either ends in square brackets naming what you will do on a yes, which EVY shows as a button, or is a question the person answers by typing; anything else goes in worth_a_look. Brackets never ask for input: a choice or a pick is a question. What the person must look at goes in `outputs` as a file, or in the result as a table or list, never only as a path in `for_the_next_agent`. Details come as short bullets, never one block. Visual results come as a list, in order, never as one page that holds them all. Each design version is its own self-contained HTML file, with its styles, fonts and pictures inside it and nothing loaded from the web, and all versions sit in one folder. List each version in `outputs` as `{kind: "design", path, version, note, width}`: `version` a short name, `note` one line on what sets it apart, `width` desktop or phone. On a pick between versions, put `recommended: true` and `why` (one plain sentence on why) on the one version you recommend, never on two. A screenshot is `{kind: "screenshot", path, name, side}` with `side` before or after; an image `{kind: "image", path, name, note}`, `name` two or three words and `note` one line on it; a document `{kind: "document", path, name}`. When your files are not on the person's Mac, pass a design's page itself as `html` instead of `path`. A code change also passes `before` and `after`, one plain sentence each on what the person saw before it and sees now. When your change is live, add the page you checked it on as `{kind: "try_it", url, name}`, so the result page opens it from Try it. A caveat about one passage of a document may quote it: give that worth_a_look line as `{text, quote}`, with `quote` the passage word for word, so the document page sets the caveat beside it in the margin. Never build a version switcher, tabs or arrows into a design: EVY builds the tabs, zoom and Pick from the list. A design report without the list gets a note back asking for one. A deck is handed over the same way: each slide its own self-contained HTML file 1280 by 720 pixels, all in one folder, listed in order. List each slide in `outputs` as `{kind: "slide", path, name}`, `name` the slide's short title, or with its page as `html` when your files are not on the person's Mac. When you also made a PowerPoint file, list it beside them as `{path, name}`. Never build arrows or a slide counter into a deck: EVY shows one slide at a time with its own strip and makes the PDF from the slides. When the person replies and you go on, end the turn with a new report: it replaces the last one and its Still open list, so leave out what they say is done. A report with no headline is refused, and one whose lines for the person carry developer words goes back once, naming them. Each needs_you line is an instruction that starts with a verb and names what the person will see when they open it, never a task's name or handle. Give each one sentence on why only a person can do it, as `why` in its object form. Say what it opens in `opens`: one choice is `{kind: "choice", options: [{label, note}], recommended, why}` with `recommended` the label you advise and `why` one plain sentence on why; a few answers to type or say are `{kind: "questions", questions: [{text, hint}]}`; steps only the person can take are `{kind: "steps", steps: [{text, note, url}]}`; a list to choose from is `{kind: "list", source, items: [{label, note, picked}]}`, with `source` the path of the output or the id of the note it comes from and `picked` true on your own picks. A line that asks the person to pick from a list, a shortlist or several candidates carries every item in that list, so they can tick them in EVY; a report with such a line and no items goes back. A pick between design versions needs no `opens`: the outputs list is what it opens. `context` is one sentence on what the task was for, for someone who no longer remembers it. An ask with a task's handle or with no reason goes back once. Before you report, check the open asks on finished results in your group: a needs-you line still open on a result in Review. `get_task` shows them under outcome.decide. Name the ones this session settled in `settles`, a list of `{task, line}`: the task id or handle, and the ask's text or its index. EVY clears each named line and keeps it under cleared, saying this result settled it. When the person answered an ask in this session's chat instead of the button, this report settles it the same way. A run that stops before it is done reports blocked. Its headline says in plain words where it stopped (the step), why, and that the work so far is kept. Keep your checklist current up to the step you stopped at, so the stop shows on it, and list what you made so far in `outputs`; the technical text goes in `details`. When only the person can unblock it (a sign-in ran out, a payment, access you lack), give one needs_you line: the step to take, with its link in `opens` as `{kind: "steps", steps: [{text, url}]}`, its reason, and the answer that says it is done in square brackets, such as [Signed in, carry on]; that answer resumes you where you stopped. Any other stop gets no needs_you line: EVY offers Run again, so never ask the person to run it again.

## Keeping EVY current
- The person decided something with you: `record_decision`, what and why, at most 40 words, once per decision. EVY keeps the first of two that say the same within the hour, and the decision needs no "Decided:" line in `update_progress` as well.
- Where the space stands changed: `update_progress` with the whole summary, at most 150 words. Start from the summary in your context and change only what changed. EVY turns away anything longer.

## Writing for EVY
- One plain fact per line: what changed, what was decided and why, what comes next.
- No account of the work itself, no restating the task, no headings or bold inside a line.
- Facts that are not EVY's (code, documents) belong in the project's own files.
